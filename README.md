## What this is

When AI removes the coordination work that white-collar institutions were built around, it does not close the gap between deployment and defensible governance. It reveals it. This book is about the human standing in that gap: the named person who authorized a deployment, who has to understand what they authorized and who will carry the consequence no matter how many automated layers sit between the authorization and the outcome.

It is a practitioner's book. It rests on thirty-five years of governance, risk management and compliance work across law, in-house counsel, trade compliance and risk management, and four years of daily AI practice on top of that. It argues for governed AI built like an honest tool rather than a magic trick. Human-made, for Easy Work, Simply Done.

The book is released free and for non-commercial use.

## The argument in three doctrines

Slow AI: AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit.

Informed Intent: no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions.

Final Liability rests with the Human: every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene.

Each statement above is the sanctioned short form of [DOCTRINE.md](https://github.com/rolldabones/rolldabones/blob/main/DOCTRINE.md#8-short-forms), the account's single normative statement; the manuscript develops them at length. The three are not separable. Slow AI produces the evidence that makes Final Liability assignable. Final Liability creates the incentive to build the governance Slow AI requires. Informed Intent closes the gap the other two leave open.

## How the book is organized

The manuscript runs to twenty-two chapters across six parts, with a Conclusion and an Epilogue.

- Part I, The Problem. Chapters 1 to 5.
- Part II, The Doctrine. Chapters 6 to 9.
- Part III, The Principal Was Always Human. Chapters 10 to 16.
- Part IV, The Contract Was Always the Workflow. Chapters 17 and 18.
- Part V, The Evidence Problem. Chapters 19 to 21.
- Part VI, The Governance Test. Chapter 22.
- Conclusion and Epilogue.

The reading index in [manuscript/README.md](manuscript/README.md) lists every chapter with its title.

Appendices D, E, F, H, K, L and N are included in the manuscript. They are working instruments rather than supplements: an assumption register, an agent registry, a Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment template, the Why Slow AI essay, the General-Purpose AI Risk Management: Adopter's Checklist, the GRCnext™ doctrine and a crosswalk of the book's question sets. A glossary and index close the manuscript as back matter. Appendices I (AI Audit and Due Diligence Checklist), J (AI GRC Master Reference) and M (GRC Workbook) are maintained as separate documents.

## How it was written

This book argues that AI must be used with discipline, with Informed Intent and with a named human carrying Final Liability for what it produces. It was written that way. The author worked heavily with AI to draft, structure, stress-test and refine the manuscript, under intent specifications he wrote and against evidentiary standards he set. Every chapter and every line passed through his judgment before it appeared. The AI was a tool. The doctrine governing its use, and the responsibility for the result, are his.

A short song that compresses the three doctrines, with lyrics by the author and an AI-generated rendering, is described in the Preface.

## A note on accuracy

The book leans on doctrine that does not depend on which regulation is in force this season. Where it cites current law, the regulatory and case claims were independently verified in the July 2026 revision and corrected since, as the table records. The EU AI Act status was re-checked on 30 July 2026: the Digital Omnibus on AI amending Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 was adopted as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026, published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 (OJ L, 2026/1744, 24.7.2026) and in force since 27 July 2026. It defers stand-alone (Annex III) high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 and obligations for AI embedded in Annex I regulated products to 2 August 2028. Those deferrals are now binding law. The Act's general application date of 2 August 2026 is unchanged. The appendix instruments state their own reference frameworks in their headers.
